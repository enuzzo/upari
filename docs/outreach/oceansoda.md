# OceanSODA-ETHZ: ocean acidification

**To:** Dr Luke Gregor and Prof. Nicolas Gruber, Environmental Physics, ETH Zürich (the
addresses are on the group's pages at ETH and in Gregor and Gruber 2021, *Earth System
Science Data* 13, 777–808).
**Subject:** Permission to show OceanSODA-ETHZ pH on Upari, a free open-source globe

---

Dear Dr Gregor, dear Professor Gruber,

I make Upari (https://upari.earth), a 3D globe that tells the Earth as it is today. It is
free, with no ads, no accounts, no tracking and no paid tier, and its code is open source
under the Apache License 2.0 (https://github.com/enuzzo/upari). It shows only open data, and
every source is credited on the page that shows it.

Upari has chapters on the warming sea, marine heatwaves and coral bleaching, and the
missing one is the sea that turns more acidic. OceanSODA-ETHZ is the dataset that tells it
best: global, monthly, from 1982, built from observations. NCEI distributes it with a
citation request but without a licence, and since Upari carries the name of my small design
studio as a signature, I would rather ask than assume.

May Upari show OceanSODA-ETHZ, with this credit on the page?

> Ocean pH and its change since 1985: OceanSODA-ETHZ (Gregor and Gruber 2021, ESSD 13,
> 777–808), ETH Zürich, via NOAA NCEI.

What the reader would see: surface pH (or the change since 1985) as a colour layer on the
globe, one frame per year, with a short note on what the data are (a machine-learning
reconstruction guided by SOCAT observations, with its uncertainties). The picture would be
pre-rendered once into a small image and committed to the repository; the original files
would not be redistributed or downloadable from Upari.

If a standard licence is simpler for you, CC BY 4.0 would cover this use exactly. A one-line
reply is enough, and I will record it, with the date, in the project's third-party notices.

Thank you for this work.

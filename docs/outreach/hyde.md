# HYDE 3.5: twelve thousand years of people

**To:** Dr Kees Klein Goldewijk, Copernicus Institute of Sustainable Development, Utrecht
University (address on the university's staff page), with the HYDE team at PBL in copy.
**Subject:** Licence of HYDE 3.5 for Upari, a free open-source globe

Dear Dr Klein Goldewijk,

I make Upari (https://upari.earth), a 3D globe that tells the Earth as it is today. It is
free, with no ads, no accounts, no tracking and no paid tier, and its code is open source
under the Apache License 2.0 (https://github.com/enuzzo/upari). It shows only open data, and
every source is credited on screen, with a link to its holder, wherever its data appear.

Upari shows where people live today and how cities grew since 1975. HYDE would let it tell
the longest story of all: from ten thousand years before our era to now, people spreading
over the continents and fields replacing forests. I could not find the licence of HYDE 3.5
next to the files, so I would rather ask than assume.

May Upari show HYDE 3.5 population density (and, if possible, cropland and pasture), with
this credit on the page?

> People and land through time: HYDE 3.5, Klein Goldewijk et al., Utrecht University and
> PBL Netherlands Environmental Assessment Agency.

What the reader would see: a sequence of epochs as coloured layers on the globe, aggregated
to about 10 km, pre-rendered once and committed to the repository as images. The original
files would not be redistributed or downloadable from Upari, and the text would say that the
deep past is a model built from historical estimates, not a measurement.

If a standard licence is simpler for you, CC BY 4.0 would cover this use exactly. A one-line
reply is enough, and I will record it, with the date, in the project's third-party notices.
